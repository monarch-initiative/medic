# Comprehensive Research Report on Drug Therapies for Achondroplasia

Achondroplasia represents the most common form of skeletal dysplasia in humans, affecting approximately one in 25,000 live births, and is caused by gain-of-function mutations in the fibroblast growth factor receptor 3 (FGFR3) gene that result in excessive suppression of endochondral bone growth and development of disproportionate short stature accompanied by significant medical complications[5][5]. Recent therapeutic advances have fundamentally transformed the treatment landscape for this previously untreatable condition, shifting from purely symptomatic management and surgical interventions to precision pharmacological therapies that directly target the underlying pathophysiology of FGFR3 overactivation. This comprehensive report synthesizes current evidence regarding approved drug therapies, investigational compounds in clinical development, drug repurposing strategies, and emerging combination therapy approaches for achondroplasia, with particular emphasis on the mechanisms of action, clinical efficacy data, safety profiles, and regulatory status of each therapeutic strategy.

## Approved Pharmacological Therapies for Achondroplasia

### Vosoritide (VOXZOGO): The First FDA-Approved Targeted Therapy

Vosoritide, marketed as VOXZOGO by BioMarin Pharmaceutical, represents a landmark achievement as the first and, until very recently, the only FDA-approved pharmacological therapy specifically designed to target the underlying pathophysiology of achondroplasia[4][27]. Vosoritide is a recombinant C-type natriuretic peptide (CNP) analog that binds to and activates the natriuretic peptide receptor type B (NPR-B), located on chondrocytes within the growth plate, thereby stimulating endochondral bone growth through a mechanism distinct from growth hormone therapy[2][17]. The molecular mechanism of action operates by augmenting CNP signaling alongside the body's natural CNP production to increase the relative balance between the normally overactive FGFR3 pathway and the suppressed CNP pathway that characterizes achondroplasia[2]. By increasing CNP signaling through NPR-B receptor activation, vosoritide initiates a cascade that inhibits downstream overactivation of the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathway that is constitutively elevated in achondroplasia, thereby disinhibiting the blocked chondrocyte hypertrophy and differentiation that normally occurs during endochondral ossification[36][14].

The FDA granted accelerated approval to vosoritide on November 19, 2021, based on a pivotal Phase 3 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial that enrolled 121 children aged 5 to 17 years with genetically confirmed achondroplasia[39]. In this landmark trial, children receiving daily subcutaneous vosoritide injections at 15 μg/kg demonstrated a statistically significant improvement in annualized growth velocity (AGV) of 1.57 cm per year compared to placebo over the 52-week treatment period[17][27]. Importantly, vosoritide represents the first approval of an accelerated approval for achondroplasia, meaning continued approval is contingent upon verification and description of clinical benefit in confirmatory trials[1][17]. Regulatory approval extends across multiple jurisdictions: vosoritide is indicated in the United States to increase linear growth in pediatric patients with achondroplasia aged 5 years and older with open epiphyses, in the European Union for children aged 4 months and older, in Australia for all ages with open growth plates, and in Japan for children from birth with open growth plates[4][17][31].

The efficacy of vosoritide has been demonstrated through multiple clinical trials with extensive long-term follow-up data. In the Phase 3 open-label extension study, participants who received vosoritide for two years demonstrated sustained improvements in annualized growth velocity, with velocities increasing from 4.26 cm/year at baseline to 5.39 cm/year at 52 weeks and maintaining at 5.52 cm/year at week 104[41]. Notably, real-world evidence from multiple countries corroborates the clinical trial findings: children in Japan aged under 2 years receiving vosoritide demonstrated mean height changes from baseline of 9.91 cm at 12 months and 15.62 cm at 24 months[4]. A French real-world study documented that children receiving vosoritide for 36 months showed mean height gains of 16.7 cm with a 0.75 increase in height Z-score compared to the average population[4]. Additionally, the VISTA study conducted in the United States demonstrated that real-world effectiveness data were consistent with clinical trial findings[4]. Long-term follow-up data through six years of continuous treatment with up to 464.05 person-years of exposure demonstrates sustained growth-promoting effects with a favorable safety profile[44].

Early initiation of vosoritide therapy appears to be particularly important for optimizing clinical outcomes. BioMarin has presented data showing that among more than 20 children who began treatment between 0.5 to less than 2 years of age, the average additional height gain after four years of treatment was 4.7 cm (p=0.0002) compared to untreated children, with an average height Z-score improvement of 0.8 (p=0.0008)[4]. These findings have led to international consensus guidelines recommending initiation of vosoritide as early as possible, with vosoritide now representing the only approved treatment for children with achondroplasia starting from birth[4]. The impact has been substantial: vosoritide has now helped more than 5,000 infants and children in more than 50 countries[4].

Beyond linear growth, emerging evidence suggests vosoritide may provide benefits in skeletal proportionality and other health outcomes relevant to achondroplasia complications. Recent data presentations at the 2026 American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics meeting demonstrate that early vosoritide treatment leads to durable and sustained improvements in skeletal growth-related health outcomes including proportionality, arm span, and body mass index[4]. Specifically, children who switched from placebo to vosoritide at Week 52 experienced improvements in the upper-to-lower body segment ratio from -0.02 at Week 52 to -0.10 at Week 104, demonstrating continued improvement in body proportionality during the second year of treatment[16]. The arm span-to-standing height ratio remained consistent with untreated participants, suggesting preservation of normal body proportions[44].

The safety profile of vosoritide has been extensively characterized across diverse age groups and treatment durations. The most commonly reported adverse events are injection site reactions, including erythema, swelling, urticaria, and pain, which are generally transient and mild[2][28]. In the pivotal Phase 3 study of children aged 5-18 years, the most common adverse reactions occurring in greater than 10% of participants included injection site erythema, injection site swelling, vomiting, injection site urticaria, arthralgia (joint pain), decreased blood pressure, and gastroenteritis[28]. A recent pharmacovigilance analysis using the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS) database identified that the most frequently reported adverse events were related to endocrine disorders including altered growth hormone levels and glucose homeostasis issues, though these signals require further investigation for clinical significance[11][11][11].

Notably, vosoritide carries the potential serious adverse effect of hypotension, and patients and caregivers should be informed to monitor for symptoms of low blood pressure including dizziness, fatigue, and nausea, with instructions to contact healthcare providers if such symptoms occur[1][2][28]. A Phase 2 study in very young children (aged 3-59 months) demonstrated generally mild adverse event profiles: all 75 participants experienced adverse events with annual rates of 204.5 adverse events per patient in the vosoritide group versus 73.6 per patient in the placebo group, with most being transient injection-site reactions and erythema[12][12][12]. Serious adverse events occurred in three vosoritide recipients (7%) including decreased oxygen saturation, respiratory syncytial virus bronchiolitis with sudden infant death syndrome, and pneumonia, compared to six placebo recipients (19%), indicating that serious adverse events were not disproportionately associated with vosoritide[12]. The long-term safety profile through six years of continuous treatment shows no new adverse effects, no long-term harms, and no deaths observed[44].

### Navepegritide (YUVIWEL): Once-Weekly CNP Prodrug with Recent FDA Approval

Navepegritide, marketed as YUVIWEL by Ascendis Pharma and developed under the designation TransCon CNP, represents a major therapeutic advance as the first and only approved once-weekly treatment for achondroplasia, offering substantially improved convenience compared to daily injection regimens[1][1]. YUVIWEL received FDA approval under the Accelerated Approval Program on February 27, 2026, making it the second approved pharmacological therapy for achondroplasia[1][1]. Navepegritide is a prodrug of C-type natriuretic peptide administered once weekly via subcutaneous injection, specifically designed to provide continuous systemic exposure of active CNP over the weekly dosing interval to counteract the overactive FGFR3 signaling characteristic of achondroplasia[1][3][1]. This once-weekly formulation represents a significant advance in treatment convenience compared to the daily injection requirement of vosoritide.

The regulatory approval of navepegritide is based on evaluation of three randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trials with up to three years of open-label extension data[1][1]. The pivotal ApproaCH trial enrolled 84 children with achondroplasia aged 2-11 years comparing once-weekly subcutaneous navepegritide (100 μg/kg) to placebo for 52 weeks, followed by an open-label extension wherein all participants received navepegritide through week 104[1][16][35][37][37]. The trial met its primary endpoint, demonstrating superiority of navepegritide with an annualized growth velocity improvement of 1.49 cm/year compared to placebo (95% CI, 1.05 to 1.93; P < .001)[35][37][37]. This represents a substantial treatment effect, and notably is lower than some other investigational agents but achieved with excellent tolerability and convenience of once-weekly dosing[36].

Beyond linear growth effects, the ApproaCH trial demonstrated improvements in additional skeletal outcomes and health-related quality of life measures. Radiographically assessed skeletal outcomes showed significant improvements including tibial-femoral angle (-1.81° [-3.16 to -0.47]), mechanical axis deviation (-2.78 mm [-4.71 to -0.86]), and fibula to tibia length ratio (-0.016 [-0.024 to -0.008])[35][37][37]. Health-related quality of life assessments using Achondroplasia Child Experience Measures showed improvements in physical functioning, particularly pronounced in children younger than 5 years (-11.1 [-21.5 to -0.80])[35][37][37]. Two-year data from the ApproaCH trial presented at the 2026 American College of Medical Genetics meeting demonstrate continued improvement in body proportionality and sustained increases in linear growth, with children switching from placebo to navepegritide at Week 52 achieving one-year results that mirrored those in the randomized double-blind phase[16].

The safety profile of navepegritide demonstrates particularly favorable tolerability. Throughout two years of treatment in the ApproaCH trial, navepegritide was generally well-tolerated, with most adverse events being mild or moderate in severity and none leading to treatment discontinuation or withdrawal[16]. Critically, there were no occurrences of symptomatic hypotension despite hypotension being a known risk with CNP-based therapies, and injection-site reaction rates were remarkably low at 0.35 per person-year of exposure[16]. Among 84 children enrolled, 80 completed the trial and all 80 enrolled into the long-term open-label AttaCH extension trial, demonstrating excellent retention and participant acceptance[16]. No serious adverse events were treatment-related, and no deaths occurred[35][37][37].

YUVIWEL carries FDA-approved labeling indicating use to increase linear growth in children 2 years of age and older with achondroplasia with open growth plates (epiphyses), with indication based on acceleration approval contingent on verification and description of clinical benefit in confirmatory trials[1][1]. Commercial availability in the United States is expected during the early part of Q2 2026[1][1]. In conjunction with this approval, the FDA issued a Rare Pediatric Disease Priority Review Voucher (PRV), which confers priority review to a subsequent drug application that would not otherwise qualify for priority review, designed to encourage development of new drugs and biologics for rare pediatric diseases[1][1]. Ascendis plans to offer a suite of patient services through its U.S. Ascendis Signature Access Program (A.S.A.P.), including support navigating treatment and financial assistance programs for eligible patients[1].

## Investigational and Pipeline Pharmacological Therapies

### Infigratinib (Truseltiq): FGFR3-Selective Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitor

Infigratinib, developed by BridgeBio Pharma (QED Therapeutics) and marketed as Truseltiq, represents a fundamentally different therapeutic approach to achondroplasia compared to CNP-based therapies, functioning as a selective oral small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitor targeting FGFR1, FGFR2, and FGFR3[20][22][25]. Unlike CNP analogs that work downstream of FGFR3 to compensate for its overactivity, infigratinib directly inhibits the ATP-binding pocket of activated FGFR3, preventing autophosphorylation and subsequent downstream signaling cascade activation[36][43]. This mechanism permits dual pathway inhibition: suppression of both the MAPK pathway (which disinhibits chondrocyte hypertrophy and differentiation) and the STAT1 pathway (which disinhibits the block on chondrocyte proliferation in achondroplasia)[36][43]. Theoretically, this dual pathway inhibition aims to normalize FGFR3 signaling toward levels observed in non-mutated, healthy chondrocytes[36].

Infigratinib has progressed through extensive clinical evaluation via the PROPEL program, which includes the PROPEL natural history study and the PROPEL 2 dose-finding Phase 2 study. The PROPEL study (NCT04035811) is a prospective, noninterventional clinical study designed to characterize the natural history and collect baseline data of children with achondroplasia over 6-24 months, serving as a cohort source for interventional studies[22][25]. PROPEL 2 (NCT04265651) represents a Phase 2, open-label, multicenter, dose-finding and dose-expansion study evaluating safety, tolerability, and efficacy of oral infigratinib in children aged 3-11 years with achondroplasia who completed at least 6 months in PROPEL[22][25][43].

The PROPEL 2 dose-escalation phase revealed compelling dose-dependent efficacy improvements. The first three cohorts at doses of 0.016, 0.032, and 0.064 mg/kg/day did not show significant increases in annualized height velocity and were assessed as non-efficacious[43]. However, at cohort 4 dose of 0.128 mg/kg/day, children ≥5 years old showed an increase in annualized height velocity from baseline of 1.52 cm/year (n=11; p=0.02)[43]. The pivotal cohort 5 dose of 0.25 mg/kg/day resulted in a statistically significant mean increase from baseline in annualized height velocity of 3.03 cm/year (p=0.0022) at month 6, representing one of the strongest single-agent effects documented in achondroplasia therapeutics[43]. Among children considered responders (defined as ≥25% improvement in annualized height velocity from baseline), the mean change in annualized height velocity was +3.81±1.8 cm/year with a median of +4.14 cm/year[43]. At month 18 follow-up, infigratinib at 0.25 mg/kg/day demonstrated a sustained mean change from baseline in annualized height velocity of +2.50 cm/year (p=0.001)[46].

The PROPEL 2 safety data demonstrated a favorable tolerability profile. Infigratinib was well tolerated with no serious adverse events or adverse events leading to study discontinuation, with most adverse events being mild or moderate in severity[43]. Critically, at the cohort 5 dose level of 0.25 mg/kg/day, no Grade 3 adverse events or treatment-related adverse events were reported[43]. These encouraging results led to advancement to Phase 3 evaluation. The PROPEL 3 trial (NCT06164951) represents a pivotal, one-year, 2:1 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 3 study conducted globally (excluding Japan) evaluating efficacy and safety of infigratinib in children with achondroplasia aged 3 to less than 18 years with open growth plates[30][9].

BridgeBio announced positive topline results from PROPEL 3 in February 2026[30]. The trial successfully met its primary endpoint of change from baseline in annualized height velocity at Week 52 (p<0.0001)[30][9]. Notably, PROPEL 3 demonstrated "the first statistically significant improvements in body proportionality in achondroplasia" among all achondroplasia therapies evaluated[9][9]. These results indicate the potential of infigratinib as a new oral option for achondroplasia, and BridgeBio plans to meet with regulatory authorities to discuss submission for regulatory approval in the United States and European Union in the second half of 2026[30].

Infigratinib has received significant regulatory recognition from the FDA including Breakthrough Therapy Designation, Orphan Drug Designation, Fast Track Designation, and Rare Pediatric Disease Designation for achondroplasia[46]. The Breakthrough Therapy Designation was granted based on results from PROPEL 2 meeting the FDA's criteria of potentially demonstrating substantial improvement in efficacy over available therapies on clinically significant endpoints[46]. If approved, infigratinib would qualify for a Priority Review Voucher, further incentivizing its development[46]. A notable advantage of infigratinib as an oral small-molecule therapy is the elimination of injection site reactions and associated treatment-related anxiety compared to injectable therapies[36].

BridgeBio has also established international partnerships to advance infigratinib development. In February 2024, BridgeBio granted Kyowa Kirin an exclusive license to develop and commercialize infigratinib for skeletal dysplasias including achondroplasia and hypochondroplasia in Japan[20][30]. In exchange, BridgeBio received an upfront payment of $100 million with royalties up to the high-twenties percent on sales and potential additional milestone-based payments[20]. Kyowa Kirin initiated the AOBA Phase 3 clinical trial in Japan in November 2025 (jRCT2031240562) to evaluate KK8398 (infigratinib) in pediatric achondroplasia patients[30].

### TYRA-300: FGFR3-Selective Small-Molecule Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitor

TYRA-300 represents another FGFR3-selective small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitor in clinical development for achondroplasia. The FDA approved an Investigational New Drug application for TYRA-300 in October 2024, clearing the way for initiation of clinical trials[3]. Preclinical evidence demonstrates that TYRA-300 treatment increases nasoanal length and tibia and femur length in mouse models of FGFR3-driven chondrodysplasia, with partial restoration of skeletal disproportionality in models carrying FGFR3 mutations[15]. Notably, TYRA-300 treatment increases both proliferation and differentiation of chondrocytes within the growth plate, mechanistically increasing bone growth through both pathways[15].

Beyond growth effects, TYRA-300 demonstrates potential to address serious complications of achondroplasia. Children with achondroplasia frequently experience foramen magnum stenosis leading to spinal cord compression and medical complications including sleep apnea, respiratory disorders, and potentially sudden death[23]. TYRA-300 treatment significantly improved the size and shape of the skull and foramen magnum in mouse models[15]. Similarly, spinal stenosis represents a frequent complication requiring surgical intervention; TYRA-300 increased lumbar vertebrae length and improved the shape of intervertebral discs in both FGFR3-altered mouse models[15]. These preclinical findings suggest TYRA-300 may address not only growth but also structural complications contributing to serious morbidity and mortality in achondroplasia.

SAR442501 represents another investigational therapeutic approach to achondroplasia currently in Phase 2 clinical development. A Phase 2, open-label, multicenter study (NCT06067425) is evaluating safety, tolerability, and efficacy of SAR442501 in children from birth up to 12 years of age with achondroplasia[10][10]. Limited additional clinical data are currently available for this agent in the literature provided.

### RBM-007: Anti-FGF2 RNA Aptamer

RBM-007 represents a novel biological approach to achondroplasia treatment, functioning as an inhibitory RNA aptamer against fibroblast growth factor 2 (FGF2)[29]. The compound consists of 37 nucleotides with 2' position modifications on ribose to resist ribonucleases, conferring an advantageous pharmacokinetic profile with 5' PEGylation and 3' inverted dT conjugation[29]. The therapeutic rationale is based on the observation that while 99% of achondroplasia cases harbor the G380R FGFR3 mutation in the transmembrane domain, the clinical applicability of direct FGFR3 inhibitors is complicated by the necessity of maintaining FGFR3 inhibition for 12-14 years from childhood through closure of growth plates[29].

RBM-007 operates by neutralizing FGF ligands that activate the mutated FGFR3, thus neutralizing the initiating signal of the pathogenic pathway. Preclinical evidence demonstrates that RBM-007 restores the proliferation arrest, degradation of cartilaginous extracellular matrix, and premature senescence attributable to FGFR3 signaling activation in cultured chondrocytes[29]. In cartilage xenografts derived from induced pluripotent stem cells of achondroplasia patients, RBM-007 restored aberrant chondrocyte differentiation and maturation in growth plate cartilage[29]. Subcutaneous injection of RBM-007 restored defective bone growth in mouse models of achondroplasia[29]. Consequently, neutralization of the FGF ligand by RNA aptamer represents a viable approach to treat skeletal dysplasia caused by ectopic FGFR3 activation[29]. Two clinical trials with RBM-007 are ongoing: Phase 2 trials in wet age-related macular degeneration and Phase 1 trials in achondroplasia[29].

## Drug Repurposing and Off-Label Therapeutic Approaches

### Meclizine Hydrochloride: Over-the-Counter Motion Sickness Drug Repurposing

Meclizine hydrochloride, an over-the-counter antihistamine commonly used for motion sickness, has emerged as an unexpected candidate for achondroplasia treatment through a drug repurposing strategy[7][42]. Researchers identified that meclizine inhibits the fibroblast growth factor receptor 3 (FGFR3) gene, and in mouse models of achondroplasia, meclizine at doses of 1 and 2 mg/kg/day promoted bone growth[7][42]. A Phase 1b clinical trial evaluated the safety and pharmacokinetics of meclizine in children with achondroplasia aged 5-10 years following 14-day repeated dosing[7][42]. Twelve patients received meclizine 12.5 mg/day (cohort 1) or 25 mg/day (cohort 2) administered after meals for 14 days, with assessment of adverse events and pharmacokinetic profiles[7][42].

The Phase 1b trial demonstrated excellent safety: no patient experienced serious adverse events in either cohort[7][42]. Pharmacokinetic analysis revealed that after 14-day repeated dosing, the area under the curve (AUC₀₋₆ₕ) after final administration was 1.5 times that after the initial dose[7][42]. Compartment models demonstrated that plasma concentration of meclizine achieved steady state on approximately the 14th day of administration[7][42]. For the regimen of meclizine 12.5 mg in patients less than 20 kg and 25 mg in patients 20 kg or greater, the average AUC₀₋₂₄ₕ was 1270 (1100-1440) ng·h/mL[7][42]. Based on these favorable Phase 1b results, long-term administration of meclizine 12.5 or 25 mg/day is recommended for Phase 2 clinical trials in children with achondroplasia[7][42]. The significant advantage of meclizine would be its extremely low cost as an established over-the-counter medication, potentially making treatment accessible to resource-limited populations.

### Recombinant Human Growth Hormone (rhGH): Traditional Symptomatic Therapy with Limited Efficacy

Recombinant human growth hormone represents the longest-established pharmacological approach to achondroplasia, though its use remains limited and not recommended as routine therapy[5][14][27]. Growth hormone is an anabolic hormone involved in synthesis of nucleic acids and proteins, stimulation of cell division, and regulation of carbohydrate metabolism, resulting in growth of organs and long bones and weight gain[14]. Recombinant somatotropin aims to enhance growth in achondroplasia patients via direct action or through insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1) effects on chondrocyte proliferation[14][27].

Clinical evidence for rhGH in achondroplasia demonstrates modest short-term benefits but limited long-term efficacy. A 1997 study testing recombinant human growth hormone in 15 children with achondroplasia showed that treatment led to increased growth rate, particularly in children with lowest height velocity before treatment[27]. However, this increase became progressively smaller during the second year of treatment, and there was no documented increase in the children's final heights[27]. A 2000 study investigating long-term effects in 145 prepubertal children showed growth hormone therapy increased growth rate and height Z-score in a dose-dependent manner, also increased serum IGF-1, IGF-binding protein-3, and osteocalcin levels with no adverse effects[27]. Historical reports documented that recombinant human growth hormone treatment for approximately 10 years resulted in mean growth gains of +3.5 cm in men and +2.8 cm in women[14]. When combined with L-thyroxine, final growth increase reached +10.0 cm in males and +9.8 cm in females[14]. Combining rhGH with surgical tibial and/or femoral elongation increased final height by +17.2 cm and +17.3 cm in males and females, respectively[14].

However, the efficacy of rhGH remains limited, and children with achondroplasia are not deficient in growth hormone, making rhGH a non-precision therapy[27]. A meta-analysis of recombinant human growth hormone treatment in achondroplasia based on an extensive group of 558 patients shows ambiguous data regarding body proportionality in GH treatment[14][14][14]. To date, it has not been confirmed whether rhGH administration negatively affects the severity of foramen magnum narrowing and spinal cord pressure, though no symptoms of acromegaly have been observed in treated patients[14][14][14]. The use of rhGH to treat achondroplasia is not routinely recommended, and recombinant human growth hormone is currently approved only in Japan for achondroplasia treatment[36]. In recent systematic reviews and treatment guidelines, rhGH is considered substantially inferior to emerging targeted therapies[36][14].

### Parathyroid Hormone (PTH 1-34/Teriparatide): Experimental Therapy for Bone Metabolism

Parathyroid hormone fragment PTH(1-34), marketed as teriparatide, represents an FDA-approved osteoporosis medication that has been investigated as an experimental therapy for achondroplasia based on its ability to stimulate bone formation and regulate chondrocyte proliferation and differentiation[24]. PTH(1-34) is an FDA-approved drug that effectively treats osteoporosis by increasing bone formation through promotion of osteoblastic proliferation, differentiation, and prevention of osteoblast apoptosis[24]. PTH plays important roles in regulating both chondrogenesis and bone remodeling, and prior studies demonstrated that PTH and parathyroid hormone-related peptide (PTHrP) can alleviate retarded skeletal development in achondroplasia mice[24][32].

Preclinical studies demonstrated that systemic intermittent PTH(1-34) injection significantly rescues the lethal phenotype in thanatophoric dysplasia type II mice and substantially alleviates retarded skeleton development in achondroplasia mice[32]. PTH-treated achondroplasia mice had longer naso-anal length than achondroplasia control mice, with bone lengths of humeri and tibiae rescued to be comparable with wild-type control mice[32]. PTH treatment partially corrected premature fusion of cranial synchondroses in achondroplasia mice, suggesting potential to rescue progressive narrowing of neurocentral synchondroses that cannot be readily corrected by surgery[32]. Additionally, PTH treatment improved osteopenia and bone structure of achondroplasia mice[32]. The increased expression of PTHrP and down-regulated FGFR3 level appear responsible for these positive effects of PTH on the achondroplasia bone phenotype[32].

In a stabilized tibia fracture model in achondroplasia mice, PTH(1-34) alleviated decreased bone mass and compromised architecture[24]. Histological analysis revealed that PTH(1-34) administration increased the size of both total callus and cartilaginous callus at 14 days post-surgery in achondroplasia mice[24]. Real-time polymerase chain reaction analysis suggested that systemic PTH(1-34) accelerated initiation of chondrogenesis and chondrocyte maturation and enhanced osteogenic differentiation in the fracture callus[24]. These preclinical findings indicate that PTH(1-34) administration results in enhanced callus formation during bone fracture healing in achondroplasia, at least in part mediated by increased cartilaginous callus at early stage and promotion of bone formation in bony callus[24]. However, clinical trials of PTH(1-34) specifically for achondroplasia have not been reported in the literature provided.

## Combination Pharmacological Therapy Approaches

### TransCon CNP and TransCon hGH Combination: Evidence of Synergistic Growth Effects

The COACH trial represents the first Phase 2 clinical trial evaluating combination therapy with once-weekly TransCon CNP (navepegritide) and once-weekly TransCon hGH (lonapegsomatropin) in children with achondroplasia[19]. This proof-of-concept trial investigates whether combining a CNP-based therapy that makes growth plates receptive to growth-promoting signals with growth hormone therapy that provides growth-promoting stimulus might produce synergistic or additive effects[19]. COACH enrolled children with achondroplasia aged 2-11 years who received both TransCon CNP at 100 μg/kg/week and TransCon hGH at a starting dose of 0.30 mg/kg/week, with both agents administered once weekly via subcutaneous injection[19].

Week 52 topline results from COACH presented in January 2026 demonstrate remarkable efficacy beyond either monotherapy alone[19]. Annualized growth velocity from combination therapy exceeded the 97th percentile of average stature children, representing growth velocities substantially above those of healthy non-affected peers[19]. Critically, the achieved height Z-score improvements indicated a tripling of efficacy compared to TransCon CNP monotherapy, suggesting substantial additive or synergistic effects of combination therapy[19]. Combination therapy demonstrated benefits beyond linear growth, including improvements in body proportionality and arm span aligned with the increase in linear growth[19]. All 18 children completed 52 weeks of treatment and remained on therapy in COACH as of the latest report[19].

The safety and tolerability profile of combination therapy were consistent with monotherapies of TransCon CNP and TransCon hGH individually, with generally mild treatment-emergent adverse events and excellent tolerability[19]. Bone age remained consistent with chronological age at Week 52, suggesting appropriate skeletal maturation without acceleration[19]. These compelling results underscore the potential of TransCon CNP to become a backbone therapy for addressing the underlying biology of achondroplasia, with TransCon hGH providing complementary growth-promoting benefit[19]. The combination therapy concept represents a rational dual-agent regimen strategy that could establish a new standard of care in achondroplasia management.

In Q4 2025, Ascendis submitted a protocol and held an end-of-Phase 2 meeting with the FDA regarding a Phase 3 trial of TransCon CNP and TransCon hGH combination therapy in pediatric achondroplasia[19], indicating this approach is progressing toward potential regulatory evaluation.

## Mechanistic Understanding of Achondroplasia and Therapeutic Targeting

Understanding the molecular basis of achondroplasia is essential for appreciating the rationale behind diverse therapeutic strategies. Achondroplasia is caused by autosomal dominant gain-of-function mutations in the FGFR3 gene, with approximately 99% of cases resulting from a single nucleotide substitution at position 1138 (either G to A or G to C) that encodes a glycine-to-arginine substitution at position 380 (G380R) in the transmembrane domain of the receptor[5][14][47]. Analysis of FGFR3 mutations reveals increased signaling through multiple mechanisms including stabilization of the receptor, enhanced dimerization, and enhanced tyrosine kinase activity[5][5]. Paradoxically, this increased FGFR3 signaling profoundly suppresses proliferation and maturation of growth plate chondrocytes, resulting in decreased growth plate size, reduced trabecular bone volume, and decreased bone elongation[5][5][14].

The mechanism involves complex downstream signaling alterations. Increased FGFR3 signaling activates overexpression of signal transducer and activator of transcription 1 (STAT1), which represents a strong candidate for regulation and suppression of chondrocyte proliferation downstream of FGFR3[5]. Additionally, suppression of proliferation is mediated by activation of p107 and p130 with expression of the cell cycle inhibitor p21^Waf1/Cip1^[5][14]. FGFR3 signaling through the MAPK-bone morphogenetic protein pathway leads to premature closure of synchondrosis, associated with stimulation of bone tissue formation, resulting in narrowing of the spinal canal and foramen magnum with neurological consequences[14].

The recognition that these signaling abnormalities operate in chondrocytes throughout growth plate development while FGFR3 itself is also expressed in many other tissues explains why achondroplasia causes systemic manifestations beyond skeletal dysplasia. FGFR3 is expressed in chondrocytes and mature osteoblasts where it functions to regulate bone growth, but the variant seen in achondroplasia is expressed in tissues throughout the body and is associated with increased risk of muscular, neurological, and cardiorespiratory complications in addition to skeletal dysplasia[1][16].

This mechanistic understanding illuminates why multiple therapeutic strategies targeting different nodes of the pathway show efficacy: CNP analogs that activate alternative downstream signaling pathways can compensate for FGFR3 overactivity; tyrosine kinase inhibitors directly suppress FGFR3 catalytic activity; FGF ligand antagonists prevent FGFR3 activation; and combination therapies may provide complementary pathway modulation.

## Adverse Events, Contraindications, and Special Populations

### Serious Adverse Effects and Safety Monitoring

The most serious potential adverse effect associated with CNP-based therapies (vosoritide and navepegritide) is hypotension. Both vosoritide and navepegritide carry labeling indicating risk of low blood pressure, and patients and caregivers should monitor for symptoms including dizziness, fatigue, and nausea, with instruction to contact healthcare providers if such symptoms occur[1][2][28][1]. However, in clinical trials, symptomatic hypotension was not observed: the ApproaCH trial with navepegritide reported no occurrences of symptomatic hypotension through two years of treatment[16]. Similarly, vosoritide trials did not document symptomatic hypotension as a significant clinical problem despite its theoretical risk.

Pharmacovigilance analysis of vosoritide using the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System identified several high-signal adverse events requiring further investigation. Notably, kyphosis displayed substantially higher signals in pediatric patients (ROR = 426.31, 95% CI: 158.92-1143.87) compared to the overall cohort, and knee deformity also showed very high reporting odds ratios (ROR = 289.45, 95% CI: 106.73-783.29)[11][11][11]. These findings warrant further investigation to understand their clinical relevance and underlying mechanisms. Ear infections emerged as a signal in chronic phase use (>60 days) with ROR of 24.69 (95% CI: 7.89-77.32)[11]. These signals require careful monitoring through pharmacovigilance systems and ongoing clinical assessment.

### Accelerated Approval Requirements and Post-Marketing Surveillance

Both approved achondroplasia therapies (vosoritide and navepegritide) received FDA approval under accelerated approval pathways, which require post-marketing surveillance to verify clinical benefit. Vosoritide carries a post-marketing requirement to conduct adequate and well-controlled clinical trials to verify and describe clinical benefit, with the stipulation that if such trials fail to verify benefit or are not conducted with due diligence, the FDA may withdraw approval following hearing procedures[39]. BioMarin intends to fulfill this requirement using ongoing open-label extension studies compared to available natural history data[31]. Similarly, navepegritide continued approval is contingent upon verification and description of clinical benefit in confirmatory trials[1][1].

### Age-Specific Safety Considerations

The safety profile of achondroplasia therapies differs across age groups. In very young children (3-59 months), vosoritide demonstrated a mild adverse event profile with adverse event rates of 204.5 per patient-year in the treatment group (primarily injection-site reactions) versus 73.6 per patient-year in placebo[12][12][12]. Serious adverse events occurred at similar or higher rates in placebo-treated children, indicating that serious adverse events were not disproportionately associated with vosoritide[12]. As infants and young children represent the most vulnerable population with the greatest opportunity to impact long-term health outcomes, and as vosoritide is the only approved treatment starting from birth, particular attention to long-term safety surveillance in this population is warranted[4].

### Contraindications and Populations to Avoid

No absolute contraindications to achondroplasia therapeutics have been formally identified, though several clinical considerations warrant caution. Patients with significant baseline hypotension or conditions predisposing to hypotension should be carefully monitored for CNP-based therapies. Similarly, infigratinib as a tyrosine kinase inhibitor may carry risks in certain patient populations that will require further characterization through Phase 3 trials and post-marketing surveillance.

## Current Clinical Practice and Treatment Sequencing

As of April 2026, the achondroplasia treatment landscape has fundamentally transformed from no approved pharmacological options to multiple targeted therapies with distinct advantages and limitations. Vosoritide remains the only approved therapy for children starting from birth, offering the longest safety and efficacy data through six years of continuous treatment with more than 5,000 infants and children treated globally[4]. Navepegritide offers the significant advantage of once-weekly dosing compared to vosoritide's daily injections, potentially improving adherence and patient quality of life, with FDA approval just achieved in February 2026 and commercial availability expected in Q2 2026[1][1]. Infigratinib offers the first oral administration option with potentially superior growth effects demonstrated in PROPEL 2 (>2.5 cm/year mean improvement) and notably the first statistically significant improvements in body proportionality among achondroplasia therapies, expected to seek FDA approval in the second half of 2026[9][30][9][46].

International consensus guidelines recommend initiation of achondroplasia therapy as early as possible to maximize clinical benefit[4]. Clinical practice increasingly favors early treatment initiation, particularly with vosoritide which is approved from birth, to capitalize on the period of highest growth velocity in early childhood. The choice between therapies involves considerations of patient age, route of administration preference (injection versus oral), convenience factors (daily versus weekly dosing), and anticipated long-term compliance. Combination therapy with TransCon CNP plus TransCon hGH represents a promising emerging strategy, though this remains investigational pending Phase 3 evaluation.

## Conclusion

The therapeutic landscape for achondroplasia has undergone a dramatic transformation over the past five years, evolving from symptomatic management and surgical interventions to precision-targeted pharmacological therapies directly addressing underlying pathophysiology. Vosoritide and navepegritide represent approved CNP-based therapies providing 1.5-1.6 cm/year improvements in growth velocity with favorable long-term safety profiles. Infigratinib, an oral FGFR3-selective tyrosine kinase inhibitor, demonstrates greater growth effects (~2.5-3 cm/year) with unique benefits including oral administration and body proportionality improvements. Combination therapy approaches show remarkable promise with three-fold efficacy multiplications. Emerging strategies including TYRA-300, SAR442501, RBM-007, and meclizine repurposing represent additional investigational options. Collectively, these therapeutic advances offer unprecedented opportunities to improve growth outcomes and reduce disease-related complications for children with achondroplasia, with ongoing clinical development and surveillance to optimize efficacy and safety in this vulnerable pediatric population.
