## Drug therapies for sickle cell disease (SCD; MONDO:0011382): comprehensive research report (focus 2023–2024)

### 0) Executive summary (current understanding)
Sickle cell disease is driven by deoxygenated sickle hemoglobin (HbS) polymerization, which initiates red-cell sickling, hemolysis, inflammation, endothelial activation, and multicellular adhesion leading to vaso-occlusion and progressive organ injury. Modern pharmacotherapy targets (i) HbS polymerization/oxygen affinity, (ii) fetal hemoglobin (HbF) induction, (iii) oxidative stress, and (iv) adhesion pathways (especially P-selectin). Since late 2023, SCD also has two FDA-approved one-time autologous hematopoietic stem cell (HSC) gene therapies with curative intent (Casgevy/exa-cel and Lyfgenia/lovo-cel). (bittmann2024newinsightsin pages 3-4, barak2024currentandfuture pages 8-10)


### 1) Key concepts and definitions (current understanding)
**Vaso-occlusive crisis (VOC)/vaso-occlusive event (VOE).** Acute painful episodes due to microvascular occlusion from adhesive interactions among sickled RBCs, leukocytes, platelets, and endothelium; VOC frequency is a primary endpoint in anti-adhesion trials (e.g., P-selectin blockade). (robinson2021advancementsinthe pages 32-47, ataga2017crizanlizumabforthe pages 7-8)

**Disease-modifying medications (DMMs).** Pharmacologic agents intended to reduce SCD complications or modify pathophysiology beyond symptomatic analgesia/transfusion support. Historically hydroxyurea was the foundational DMM; newer DMMs (L-glutamine, crizanlizumab, voxelotor) expanded options, and 2024 ASH education guidance emphasizes practical selection issues and the lack of combination guidelines. (walden2024practicalguidefor pages 1-2)

**Curative-intent gene therapies.** Autologous CD34+ HSC products modified ex vivo and reinfused after myeloablative conditioning to (a) induce HbF (CRISPR/BCL11A enhancer editing; exa-cel/Casgevy) or (b) add an anti-sickling β-globin variant (HbAT87Q; lovo-cel/Lyfgenia). (barak2024currentandfuture pages 8-10, crook2024utahmedicaiddura pages 32-35)

#### 2.1 Hydroxyurea / hydroxycarbamide (approved; cornerstone DMM)
**Mechanism.** Ribonucleotide reductase inhibition with multifactorial effects including HbF induction and reduced inflammatory/adhesive cell counts; HbF delays HbS polymerization. (franceschi2025selectingpatientswith pages 2-3)

**Regulatory status (selected).** FDA approval for adults reported as 1998 with subsequent pediatric expansion (2017) in review literature; EMA-approved hydroxycarbamide product for SCD is noted in contemporary reviews. (walden2024practicalguidefor pages 1-2, bittmann2024newinsightsin pages 3-4)

**Efficacy (key evidence).** Long-term clinical experience and trial data support reductions in vaso-occlusive complications and transfusion needs; hydroxyurea remains “gold standard” despite newer agents. (franceschi2025selectingpatientswith pages 2-3, riley2024hydroxyureainthe pages 9-11)

#### 2.2 L-glutamine (Endari) (FDA-approved; oxidative stress/adhesion biology)
**Mechanism.** Increases NAD redox potential/reduced NADH in erythrocytes, likely reducing oxidative stress and possibly reducing RBC adhesiveness. (robinson2021advancementsinthe pages 32-47, ballas2020theevolvingpharmacotherapeutic pages 11-12)

**Regulatory status.** FDA approval July 7, 2017 for adults and children ≥5 years; reviews note EMA rejection. (ballas2020theevolvingpharmacotherapeutic pages 11-12, franceschi2025selectingpatientswith pages 2-3)

**Pivotal trial (Phase 3).** Median pain crises 3.0 vs 4.0 over 48 weeks and fewer hospitalizations with L-glutamine vs placebo; effect was consistent with and without concomitant hydroxyurea (no significant interaction). (robinson2021advancementsinthe pages 32-47, niihara2018aphase3 pages 6-7)

**Key statistic (from trial text).** Median time to first crisis 84 vs 54 days and to second crisis 212 vs 133 days favoring L-glutamine. (niihara2018aphase3 pages 6-7)

#### 2.3 Crizanlizumab (Adakveo) (anti–P-selectin; regulatory divergence)
**Mechanism.** Humanized monoclonal antibody that inhibits P-selectin-mediated multicellular adhesion and inflammatory signaling central to vaso-occlusion. (robinson2021advancementsinthe pages 32-47, barak2024currentandfuture pages 5-7)

**Regulatory status and recent changes.** FDA approval (Nov 15, 2019) is reported in reviews; European marketing authorization revocation is noted after confirmatory trial failure in 2023. (ballas2020theevolvingpharmacotherapeutic pages 11-12, barak2024currentandfuture pages 7-8)

**Pivotal evidence (SUSTAIN).** High-dose crizanlizumab delayed time to first crisis (median 4.07 vs 1.38 months; HR 0.50; P=0.001) and reduced annual uncomplicated crisis rate (median 1.08 vs 2.91; P=0.02). (ataga2017crizanlizumabforthe pages 7-8)

**Recent 2024 data.** SOLACE-adults final results (single-arm) reported near-complete P-selectin inhibition and a sustained median reduction in VOCs leading to healthcare visit (−0.79 to −0.98 depending on dose), with infusion-related reactions in some patients. (robinson2021advancementsinthe pages 32-47)

#### 2.4 Voxelotor (Oxbryta) (Hb oxygen-affinity modulator; withdrawn)
**Mechanism.** Binds hemoglobin and increases oxygen affinity (left-shifts P50), stabilizing oxygenated HbS and inhibiting HbS polymerization, thereby reducing hemolysis. (bittmann2024newinsightsin pages 3-4, franceschi2025selectingpatientswith pages 2-3)

**Pivotal evidence (HOPE).** Hemoglobin response (>1 g/dL at week 24) occurred in ~51% (1500 mg) vs ~7% placebo; reductions in indirect bilirubin and reticulocyte percentage supported reduced hemolysis. (robinson2021advancementsinthe pages 32-47, migotsky2022recentadvancesin pages 13-14)

**Regulatory/safety updates (2024).** The manufacturer announced on **25 Sept 2024** a voluntary worldwide withdrawal of all voxelotor stocks and discontinuation of trials/expanded access because “the overall benefit … no longer outweighs the risk.” Separately, EMA/CHMP recommended suspension of marketing authorization amid safety signals including higher mortality in some trials and registry reports of increased VOCs during treatment. (franceschi2025selectingpatientswith pages 2-3, rubio2024thecurrentrole pages 9-11)


### 3) Investigational and pipeline drugs (clinical trials, phases, NCTs)
Recent 2023–2024 pipeline activity has emphasized next-generation anti-adhesion strategies, red-cell metabolism (pyruvate kinase activation), and next-generation hemoglobin modulators.

**Selected late-stage/active pipeline agents (examples):**
- **Inclacumab** (anti–P-selectin, fully human IgG4): phase 3 trials **NCT04935879**, **NCT04927247**; safety extension **NCT05348915**. (barak2024currentandfuture pages 7-8)
- **Etavopivat (FT-4202)** (PKR activator): phase 1 published; phase 2/3 program includes **NCT04624659** (HIBISCUS) and **NCT04987489**; mechanistic rationale is increased ATP and reduced 2,3-DPG. (barak2024currentandfuture pages 5-7, saraf2024multicenterphase1 pages 16-17)
- **Mitapivat (AG-348)** (PKR activator; repurposed): phase 2/3 **NCT05031780** (RISE UP); extension/omics **NCT04610866**. (barak2024currentandfuture pages 5-7)
- **Osivelotor / GBT021601** (next-generation Hb modulator after voxelotor): adult/pediatric randomized trial **NCT05431088**; extension **NCT05632354**. (barak2024currentandfuture pages 5-7, rubio2024thecurrentrole pages 9-11)
- **Epeleuton (15-HEPE)** (omega-3 derivative; adhesion molecule downregulation): biomarker/VOC study **NCT05861453**. (barak2024currentandfuture pages 7-8)
- **Famotidine** (repurposing concept; pediatric pilot to reduce P-selectin biology): **NCT05084521**. (barak2024currentandfuture pages 7-8)
- **IVIG** (adjunct in acute VOC; immune modulation): phase 2 RCT **NCT01757418**. (barak2024currentandfuture pages 7-8)

**Example of a 2024 primary-trial outcome (pipeline statistic).** In a multicenter phase 1 study of **etavopivat**, 73.3% of treated patients had a hemoglobin increase >1 g/dL at any time during treatment, with ATP increased and 2,3-DPG decreased as expected for PK activation. (saraf2024multicenterphase1 pages 16-17)


### 4) Gene therapies (approved late 2023; real-world implementation considerations)
#### 4.1 Exagamglogene autotemcel (exa-cel; Casgevy) — FDA-approved gene editing
**Mechanism.** Ex vivo CRISPR-Cas9 editing of the erythroid-specific BCL11A enhancer in autologous CD34+ HSPCs to reactivate HbF. (barak2024currentandfuture pages 8-10)

**Regulatory milestone.** FDA approval on **8 Dec 2023** is reported in 2024 reviews and policy/implementation summaries. (rahmat2024fdaapprovalof pages 2-3, crook2024utahmedicaiddura pages 32-35)

**Key efficacy statistic (CLIMB SCD-121; NEJM 2024).** Among 30 evaluable patients, 97% were free from VOCs for ≥12 consecutive months; 100% were free from VOC-related hospitalization for ≥12 consecutive months; safety was consistent with busulfan conditioning and autologous transplant, and no cancers occurred in the reported follow-up. (barak2024currentandfuture pages 8-10)

#### 4.2 Lovotibeglogene autotemcel (lovo-cel; Lyfgenia) — FDA-approved gene addition
**Mechanism.** Lentiviral vector gene addition enabling expression of anti-sickling HbAT87Q in autologous HSPCs. (barak2024currentandfuture pages 8-10, crook2024utahmedicaiddura pages 32-35)

**Regulatory milestone.** FDA approval on **8 Dec 2023** for patients ≥12 years with a VOE history. (crook2024utahmedicaiddura pages 32-35)

**Key efficacy statistics (trial programs).** Interim summaries report ~88% VOE-free and 94% elimination of severe VOEs with median follow-up ~35.8 months across pivotal cohorts; another 2024 summary reports 28/32 (88%) achieved VOE complete resolution in a phase 1/2 study window. (crook2024utahmedicaiddura pages 35-37, rahmat2024fdaapprovalof pages 2-3)

**Safety warning (boxed/major).** Implementation summaries emphasize Lyfgenia carries a black box warning for potential increased risk of hematologic malignancy and a theoretical risk of insertional oncogenesis from the lentiviral vector; both gene therapies also carry warnings for cytopenias, mucositis/stomatitis, and engraftment complications. (crook2024utahmedicaiddur pages 35-37)


### 5) Current applications and real-world implementation
**Clinical use reality.** 2024 ASH education guidance emphasizes that access, affordability, and adherence limit real-world optimization of DMMs, and that no published guidelines specify how to select among DMMs or use multiple in combination. (walden2024practicalguidefor pages 1-2)

**Hydroxyurea remains foundational.** Even in the “modern era,” hydroxyurea remains the gold standard due to accessibility, cost, and long safety/efficacy record. (riley2024hydroxyureainthe pages 9-11)

**Gene therapy implementation.** Policy summaries highlight gene therapies require specialized centers, myeloablative conditioning, and careful toxicity and long-term malignancy monitoring; access constraints and extensive supportive care are integral to real-world delivery. (crook2024utahmedicaiddura pages 32-35, crook2024utahmedicaiddur pages 35-37)


### 6) Expert opinions and authoritative analysis (2023–2024)
**Combination/selection guidance gap.** A 2024 ASH “practical guide” explicitly notes “no published guidelines advise how best to select between DMMs or to use multiple in combination,” and points to voxelotor’s withdrawal as reinforcing unmet need and the importance of evidence-based sequencing/escalation. (walden2024practicalguidefor pages 1-2)

**Hydroxyurea’s enduring role.** A 2024 expert review concludes that despite the expanding therapeutic landscape, hydroxyurea remains the “gold standard,” highlighting underutilization, pharmacogenomics, and affordability as key themes. (riley2024hydroxyureainthe pages 9-11)


### 7) Contraindications and special-population cautions
#### 7.1 Hydroxyurea in pregnancy and lactation (caution / often avoided)
Registry evidence from the Sickle Cell Disease Implementation Consortium (SCDIC) indicates that hydroxyurea use during conception **and pregnancy** (not conception only) was associated with increased odds of miscarriage or stillbirth (OR 2.21, 95% CI 1.40–3.47) and increased odds of low birth weight in full-term infants (OR 2.98, 95% CI 1.09–7.38). (kroner2022pregnancyoutcomeswith pages 1-3)

Mechanistic rationale for caution includes hydroxyurea’s antineoplastic origin and ribonucleotide reductase inhibition with theoretical teratogenicity, supported by animal data and expert concern. (kroner2022pregnancyoutcomeswith pages 1-3)

#### 7.2 Gene therapy warnings
Lyfgenia carries a black box warning for hematologic malignancy risk with a mechanistic basis in theoretical insertional oncogenesis from its lentiviral vector; exa-cel includes a unique warning for theoretical off-target genome editing. Both share warnings for delayed/failed engraftment and severe cytopenias related to conditioning and transplantation. (crook2024utahmedicaiddur pages 35-37)

#### 7.3 Voxelotor safety-related discontinuation
Voxelotor is no longer a viable option in many settings due to late-2024 manufacturer withdrawal and regulatory safety actions (EMA CHMP suspension recommendation) following signals including increased VOCs and mortality in some data sources. (franceschi2025selectingpatientswith pages 2-3, rubio2024thecurrentrole pages 9-11)


### 8) Adverse events of special relevance (drug-induced worsening)
The retrieved evidence base primarily supports adverse-event profiles within treated SCD populations (e.g., cytopenias with myeloablation/gene therapy; infusion reactions with P-selectin antibodies), rather than identifying common non-SCD drugs that induce or worsen SCD itself. Key examples of clinically important adverse events include:
- **Crizanlizumab:** infusion-related reactions (including severe reactions in some patients) and high rates of grade ≥3 AEs in long-term single-arm data. (robinson2021advancementsinthe pages 32-47)
- **Voxelotor:** regulatory safety signals including higher mortality in some trials and registry-based reports of increased VOC frequency during treatment, prompting suspension/withdrawal actions. (rubio2024thecurrentrole pages 9-11, franceschi2025selectingpatientswith pages 2-3)
- **Gene therapies:** high rates of cytopenias, mucositis/stomatitis, febrile neutropenia; malignancy warning (Lyfgenia). (crook2024utahmedicaiddur pages 35-37)


### 9) Combination therapies (evidence and gaps)
**Established real-world pattern:** hydroxyurea is frequently used concomitantly with newer DMMs in trials and practice; many pivotal trials enrolled “the majority” on hydroxyurea. (riley2024hydroxyureainthe pages 9-11, niihara2018aphase3 pages 6-7)

**Trial-based combination evidence:**
- HOPE (voxelotor) required stable hydroxyurea dosing and stratified randomization by hydroxyurea use. (vichinsky2019aphase3 pages 2-4)
- L-glutamine phase 3 trial showed no significant interaction with hydroxyurea use (rate ratios ~0.77–0.78). (niihara2018aphase3 pages 6-7)

**Modeled synergy:** A 2022 systematic review reported modeled synergy (Loewe additivity) suggesting voxelotor + L-glutamine may be superior and that other pairings with crizanlizumab may be additive; the authors explicitly emphasize clinical trials are needed before broad adoption. (dick2022comparingthesafety pages 21-21)

**Guideline gap:** 2024 ASH education guidance explicitly states there are no published guidelines on selecting among DMMs or using multiple DMMs in combination. (walden2024practicalguidefor pages 1-2)


### 10) Recent statistics and quantitative data points (from included evidence)
- **Voxelotor (HOPE):** hemoglobin response >1 g/dL at week 24 in ~51% vs ~7% placebo; mean Hb change ~+1.1 g/dL with reduced indirect bilirubin and reticulocyte % (markers of hemolysis). (robinson2021advancementsinthe pages 32-47, migotsky2022recentadvancesin pages 13-14)
- **Crizanlizumab (SUSTAIN):** median time to first crisis 4.07 vs 1.38 months; uncomplicated crises median 1.08 vs 2.91/year (P=0.02). (ataga2017crizanlizumabforthe pages 7-8)
- **L-glutamine (Phase 3):** 25% lower cumulative painful crises over 48 weeks; time to first crisis 84 vs 54 days; no significant interaction by hydroxyurea use. (niihara2018aphase3 pages 6-7)
- **Exa-cel/Casgevy (NEJM 2024):** 97% free from VOCs for ≥12 consecutive months; 100% free from VOC-related hospitalization for ≥12 consecutive months; no cancers reported in follow-up. (barak2024currentandfuture pages 8-10)
- **Hydroxyurea in pregnancy registry:** miscarriage/stillbirth OR 2.21 with hydroxyurea during conception+pregnancy; low birth weight OR 2.98 (full-term). (kroner2022pregnancyoutcomeswith pages 1-3)


### 11) Evidence gaps and limitations (transparent)
- Regulatory labels/approval documents (FDA/EMA SmPC/label PDFs) were not directly retrieved as primary sources in the provided tool context; several regulatory details are therefore supported by peer-reviewed reviews and policy summaries rather than the original label text.
- A comprehensive list of drugs contraindicated in SCD (e.g., by precipitating hemolysis, VOC, or hyperviscosity) is not present in the retrieved evidence set; the strongest “contraindication-like” evidence here relates to pregnancy/lactation cautions (hydroxyurea) and safety-driven regulatory withdrawals (voxelotor) and boxed warnings (Lyfgenia).
